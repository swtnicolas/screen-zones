# ScreenZones

ScreenZones is a menu bar agent that splits displays into fixed zones that behave like independent monitors. Each display is enabled on its own and shows a layout of zones; a window on an enabled display belongs to one zone, keeps its own size and position inside it and never crosses into another zone; it changes zone only by a tab drag, a rule or a move to another display. Apps marked free are never tracked. Each zone has a tab bar on any of its sides. Swift 6, pure AppKit, SwiftPM only.

## Layout

- `Sources/ScreenZones/Geometry.swift` — the pure model: `Side` (the four bar sides and their menu names), `Zone` as display fractions with an optional own bar side (`tabsAt`, kept through every edit and handed to both halves of a split), `Layout` (a named set of zones; `Layout.single`, "Una columna", is the only built-in one and is never stored or edited), `PlacedZone` (full, bar and content rects in points for a bar side, abutting; `margins`, the content's offsets from the full rect; `widened`, an opened side bar; `resolve` falls back to the nearest earlier letter, so C lands in B on two columns), `walls` (the display edges another display borders along a zone), `contain`, `arrive` and `center` (all of them keep a window off its bar), divider lines (`dividers`, and `uncovered`, the lines minus the bars), divider moves (`moving`, `edgeMoves`), tab widths (`tabWidths`), splits and removals (`splitting`, `removing`; `relabel` letters zones in reading order), gesture classification and `retry`. `Tests/ScreenZonesTests` covers this file, `DisplayConfig` and `Layout` decoding, `Layout.unique` and the `Store` rules for drafts and bar sides.
- `Displays.swift` — the active displays (those mirroring no other, left to right), lookup by UUID, the name the menus show (read live, never stored), native size and the displays whose current Space is a full-screen one.
- `ZoneManager.swift` — one `Surface` per enabled display (its screen, area, bounds, the other displays' bounds and placed zones), tracked windows by `CGWindowID` with their display UUID and zone, AX notifications, mouse gestures, full screen, Space and screen changes, the bars, and the editor sessions that create or edit a layout.
- `Store.swift` — `UserDefaults` of `local.screenzones`: `displays` (JSON `DisplayConfig` per display UUID: the name of its `layout`, `draft`, its unsaved zones, and `tabsAt`, its own bar side; decoded key by key, so a missing or malformed key takes its default instead of dropping every display), `layouts` (JSON array of the user's `Layout`s, in creation order; names are unique ignoring case and never "Una columna", `Layout.unique` numbers a taken one), `rules` (JSON `Rule` per bundle ID: optional display UUID and zone), `free` (bundle IDs whose windows ignore zones; empty by default), `last` (JSON per set of enabled displays present, keyed by their sorted UUIDs: a `Rule` per bundle ID with the display and zone its windows were last in), `tabsAt` (the general bar side, default top, which a display's `tabsAt` overrides and a zone's `tabsAt` overrides in turn), the menu toggles `borders` and `lockZones` (default on), `compactTabs` and `snap` (default off), and `windows` (JSON `SavedWindow` per window ID: pid, display, zone, preferred size and filled state). A display references its layout by name and shows its `draft` when it has one, else the layout's zones (`Store.zones(of:)`). Moving a divider by resizing a window (with `lockZones` off) only sets that display's draft, dropped when it matches the layout again; the menu marks it «sin guardar» and offers to discard it, and the editor opens on it. Saving the editor changes the layout itself and drops the drafts of every display showing it, choosing a layout drops the display's draft, renaming updates the references, and removing a layout sends its displays back to "Una columna" without drafts. Setting a zone's bar side writes it into its layout and into every draft of that layout; on "Una columna" it sets the display's side.
- `ZoneEditor.swift` — the on-screen zone editor: a key panel over a display's zone area, titled with the layout's name (marked «sin guardar» when it opens on the display's draft), that shows each zone's size in points labeled px (the unit of window sizes) and percent, and its physical size on the panel (points times the display's native width over the screen's width in points), splits a zone into columns or rows, removes it (its neighbors along one whole side take its space) and drags the lines between zones, which stay where they are dropped. Return saves, Esc cancels.
- `ZoneBar.swift` — the per-zone tab bar panel: a row on top or bottom, `ZoneBar.height` tall, or a column of `rowHeight` rows on the left or right, `sideWidth` wide, `narrowWidth` with `Store.compactTabs`. A click focuses a tab; dragging it inside its own bar reorders the tabs live, dropping it elsewhere moves the window to the zone under the pointer; a secondary click on a tab opens the tab menu, and on the zone letter or an empty part of the bar the main menu with that zone's bar side on top. In a row with `compactTabs` every tab but the zone's frontmost shrinks to its icon; a narrow column shows icons only. The active tab and the tab under the pointer (`hoveredIndex`, tracked through the bar's `.mouseMoved` tracking area) draw red, yellow and green buttons at their end, after the icon and title, when the tab shows its title, is wide enough and is not minimized; they close, minimize and fill or restore that tab's window as the tab menu does, and show their symbols while the pointer is over them. A click on the zone letter toggles the zone's sizes beside the bar: zone, physical and percent, closed by any click outside that letter or a geometry change. The file also holds the drop highlight (`Ghost`), the size badge (`SizeBadge`, a click-through floating panel with one label and value per line, placed by its caller), the divider lines (`ZoneBorders`) and `FreePanel`. Tab order is `Tracked.order`, in memory only.
- `AX.swift` — `AXUIElement` helpers. `_AXUIElementGetWindow` is private API and the only way to map an AX window to a `CGWindowID`.
- `AppObserver.swift` — one `AXObserver` per regular app: window created, focus, moved and resized on the app element; destroyed, title and minimize on each tracked window. Regular apps are observed on `didLaunchApplicationNotification`, posted once they finish launching, so their AX is up within the 5 s of subscribe retries. That notification is never posted for `LSUIElement` apps, so KVO on `NSWorkspace.runningApplications` catches the rest of the launches, and every exit. Apps that are not regular yet are watched through KVO on `activationPolicy` and observed once they turn regular, as menu bar agents do when they open a window; activation is no signal, since an agent launched in the background may never become active. Their borderless overlays report `AXUnknown` and stay untracked.
- `MouseTap.swift` — an active `CGEventTap` on left down and up.
- `AppDelegate.swift` — the menus and the wait for the Accessibility grant. The status item opens the main menu: one submenu per connected display (zones on or off, its layouts, its bar side, the current layout's edit, discard, rename and remove unless it is "Una columna", "Nueva disposición…", which asks a name, then opens the editor on one column), the general bar side and `compactTabs`, borders, the zone size lock, the magnet, the free apps (unchecking one puts it back in zones), pause. Opened from a bar, the main menu starts with that zone and its bar side. The tab menu: fill the zone or go back to the previous size (as the green button does), center the window in its zone (`Geometry.center` centers a fitting axis and places an oversized one as `arrive` does), minimize, close (it focuses the window first so a save prompt shows in front, then presses its close button through AX), its zone's bar side, where the app opens (a zone per display, or "La última zona que usó", which means no rule) and freeing the app; a window whose app has no bundle identifier gets the menu without the rule and the freeing.
- `StatusIcon.swift` — the status item image, drawn in code in color (not a template): filled blocks while managing, outlines while paused or without the grant.
- `Sources/PrivateAPI` — Objective-C header declaring the SkyLight functions `CGSMainConnectionID` and `CGSCopyManagedDisplaySpaces`; the `.m` exists only because SwiftPM needs a source file.
- `tools/drive.swift` reads AX frames and posts drags and clicks to test the app; it needs the Accessibility permission of the process that runs it.
- `assets/icon/` — `AppIcon.icns` and `AppIcon.iconset/`, rendered from the repo's master `assets/icon/AppIcon.svg`. They hold 128 pt and up only: macOS draws 16 and 32 pt representations shrunk onto a plate, and scales 128 pt down cleanly.

## Build

Build on the Mac that runs the app. Gatekeeper blocks a downloaded build that Apple has not notarized, while a locally built copy carries no quarantine flag, so never ship a prebuilt bundle or a release workflow. The version is `CFBundleShortVersionString` in `Resources/Info.plist`; macOS has no tag.

`scripts/build.sh` compiles in release and assembles `build/ScreenZones.app`, signed with `Nico Local` when the keychain has it, else ad-hoc. `scripts/install.sh` also replaces `~/Applications/ScreenZones.app`, writes the launch agent `~/Library/LaunchAgents/local.screenzones.login.plist`, which opens the app at every login through `open` (so LaunchServices applies `LSEnvironment` and never starts a second instance) and is attributed to the app in Login Items through `AssociatedBundleIdentifiers`, and launches the app. `scripts/uninstall.sh` quits the app, boots out and removes the agent and removes the app; the `UserDefaults` of `local.screenzones` and the Accessibility grant stay. `scripts/test.sh` runs `swift test` with the Swift Testing plugin path the Command Line Tools need.

The Accessibility grant is bound to the designated requirement, bundle identifier plus certificate leaf, so it survives rebuilds signed with `Nico Local` and is lost on every ad-hoc build.

The menu has no quit item: `scripts/quit.sh`, which both scripts run, asks the running app to quit with the distributed notification `local.screenzones.quit` and falls back to `pkill`, whose SIGTERM skips `applicationWillTerminate`.

## Mechanisms

- Coordinates are AX/CG global points, origin at the top left of the primary display. `flip` converts to Cocoa only to place panels.
- Displays are matched by UUID, never by position: positions change with the arrangement.
- `visibleFrame` starts one point below the menu bar window (layer `kCGMainMenuWindowLevel`), which would leave a strip of wallpaper above the bars, so the zone area starts at the menu bar window's bottom and the panels override `constrainFrameRect` because AppKit otherwise pushes windows out of that strip.
- Each zone's bar takes a strip along its side (`Store.side(of:on:)`), `ZoneBar.size(of:compact:)` thick; windows live in the content rect beside it. A compact side bar opens to `sideWidth` over the window (`PlacedZone.widened`) 0.15 s after the pointer enters and closes as soon as it leaves, unless a tab is being dragged. While open it sits at the floating level, so a window that a click on one of its tabs activates never covers it and takes the pointer; it goes back to the normal level and behind every window when it closes, so the content rect never changes. Changing a side or `compactTabs` reflows every window.
- Placement (`ZoneManager.place`): every placement asks for the window's preferred size (`Tracked.preferred`: its size when adopted, then the size a resize by the user or the app leaves it with) clamped to its content rect, so a window shrunk by a smaller zone grows back in a larger one; the request is size-only so a refused size never moves it; a size the app rounds up past the request (Terminal snaps to whole rows, relative to its current size) is asked once more shortened by the overshoot (`Geometry.retry`), and a size still refused then (a minimum size) is kept in `Tracked.minimum` and requested from then on. `free`, used for the user's own moves and resizes, runs `Geometry.contain`: an edge the zone shares with another zone is a wall the window never crosses, and so is a display edge that another display borders along the zone (`Geometry.walls`, from every active display's bounds, enabled or not); past any other display edge the window stays where it was left while `Geometry.visible` points of it (all of it when smaller) remain in the zone; its top is never above the content rect unless its bar is at the bottom and the window is taller than the content rect. With the menu's magnet on (`Store.snap`), `free` runs `Geometry.arrive` without centering, and turning it on places every tracked window that way at once. Every placement ScreenZones starts on its own (`keep`, `arrive`, `fill`: launch, display changes, layout and divider changes, drops) runs `Geometry.arrive`, which sends an oversized axis to overflow past the display edge its zone touches (a left zone overflows left, any other zone right and down) and centers a fitting axis only for a window arriving from elsewhere by a rule or creation. All of them take the zone's bar side: an edge next to a bar counts as a divider, so a window never slides under its bar, and an axis larger than the content rect overflows away from the bar, past the display's top when the bar is at the bottom. Drops and reflows never center. `keep` refills a window that was filling its zone; `fill` takes the whole content rect. `Tracked.filled` is recomputed after every placement.
- Bars and divider lines are normal-level panels sent to the back with `orderBack`, behind every window. Zones abut, so a divider takes no space from windows: it is a `ZoneManager.lineWidth` gray line on the first column or row of the zone after it, drawn by a `ZoneBorders` panel wherever no bar sits (`Geometry.uncovered`) and by a bar on its own left or top edge when that edge is a divider (`leftLine`, `topLine`, off with the borders). A window flush with that zone covers the line beside its bar. `edgeMoves` subtracts the zone's `margins`, so a window edge next to a bar maps to the divider beyond the bar.
- Zone borders are one opaque, click-through panel per stretch of inner divider line (`Geometry.dividers` merges the shared edges and skips the display's outer edges), moved only when the geometry changes. A transparent overlay covering the display would make WindowServer recomposite it on every change underneath.
- Tab drags carry the window: a `.common` timer applies the latest pointer position through `kAXPosition` at most every 1/60 s, the carried window's AX notifications are ignored, and the drop places it with `keep` in the zone under the pointer.
- Gestures: moved and resized notifications that arrive while the left button is down, or within 0.4 s of its release, are collected and settled 0.15 s after the last one against the mouse-down and mouse-up points. Settling takes each window's frame from the WindowServer (`synced`) and, when the app reports another one through AX, sets that frame through AX so the app agrees again. A resize that follows a double-click inside the window (a title bar double-click zooms it) toggles filling its zone, as the green button does. A window pressed inside its frame and dragged more than `Geometry.grab` points, whose size stayed the same while the button was down (`resizedWhileDown`, against its frame at the press, `pressedFrames`) and changed after the release, was tiled by macOS: it fills its zone, or the zone under the pointer on another enabled display (`land`). Tiles from the keyboard or the Window menu arrive outside a gesture and are contained. With `lockZones` off, any other resize of a window that filled its zone, with the grabbed edge on a divider (within 12 px), moves that divider for every zone on the line of the display's draft (`movedDividers`); any other resize is contained. While the button is down, each resized notification of a tracked window shows `SizeBadge` beside the pointer (flipped at the screen edges) with its WindowServer frame: the size of every zone `movedDividers` would change, the window size, and its physical size on the panel (`panelScale`, the factor the editor uses); a move or the mouse-up hides it. A move stays in its zone unless it is released on another enabled display, which takes it, or outside every enabled display, which releases it. Outside a gesture every change is contained.
- `Tracked.known` is the frame a window ended with after its last placement. A notification carrying that frame is ignored, which keeps apps that refuse a size (minimum sizes) from looping.
- The green button: a mouse-down within 100×52 points of a tracked window's top-left corner is hit-tested through AX; a hit on `AXFullScreenButton` or `AXZoomButton` is swallowed and toggles between filling the zone and the frame the window had before, so native full screen comes only from the keyboard or the app's menu.
- Full screen shows up as a frame covering the zone area with `AXFullScreen` set. The window stays in native full screen on its own Space and ScreenZones leaves it alone (`Tracked.nativeFullScreen`) until a frame no longer covers the area: macOS ignores a frame set on a full-screen window, and an app taken out of full screen through AX also leaves its own full-screen mode (a browser's video goes back into the page). Full screen is checked on every change of a tracked window before the hold and the `known` check, and on every placement, so neither a hold nor a placement misses it: placing a window in native full screen would record the display's frame as `known`, and its later notifications would be ignored. Every Space change, and again 1 s later, checks `AXFullScreen` alone on the windows of displays showing a full-screen Space (`takeFullScreenWindows`), since the Space can switch before the window takes the display's frame. The full-screen frame equals the whole display only while the menu bar hides in full screen; with it always shown (`AppleMenuBarVisibleInFullscreen`) it starts below the menu bar.
- A display whose current Space is a full-screen one (type 4 in `CGSCopyManagedDisplaySpaces`, keyed by the UUID of the screen shown) gets no bars or borders: the panels join every Space, so they would cover the top of a full-screen window and take its clicks. It also keeps the zone area it had (`refreshGeometry`) while its size is unchanged, because its visible frame there is the full-screen Space's, without the menu bar. Entering and leaving full screen switches Spaces, and the Space change redraws.
- Bars list tracked windows present in `CGWindowListCopyWindowInfo(.optionOnScreenOnly)`, which drops minimized, hidden and other-Space windows, plus the minimized ones (`Tracked.minimized`, read once at adoption and then kept from the miniaturize notifications, so no AX query per redraw), drawn dimmed. The frontmost on-screen window is the active tab. Selecting a minimized tab or dragging it restores the window first.
- Rules apply when a window is created, at launch and when set from the menu, never on Space or screen changes, so a window dragged to another display stays there. A rule without a display goes to the leftmost enabled display that has its zone. Without a rule, a new window goes to its app's last zone for the current set of displays (`Store.lastZones`, recorded whenever a window of the app is adopted or changes zone or display, except during a hold, when the set is still settling), on the display under the window when the recorded one is not enabled; each set keeps its own memory, so working on the built-in display alone never rewrites where apps go with the external one connected; an app with neither goes to zone A of the display under the window. Free apps are never adopted.
- Every display change opens a 3 s hold: tracked windows that move are not released, and a reflow at its end puts them back. macOS evacuates a vanished display's windows to the main display, which would otherwise release them.
- Each tracked window's display, zone, preferred size and filled state go to `Store.windows` one second after a placement, a release or the launch (`scheduleSave`) and at quit, and adoption restores them for the same window ID and pid, placing the window with `keep` when its saved display is still enabled; entries no window has claimed yet are saved again while their app runs.

## Gotchas

- Tap callbacks and AX notifications run on the main thread, and an active tap holds every click system-wide until its callback returns. `start()` sets a 1 s AX messaging timeout on the system-wide element, which applies to every AX call in the process, so a hung app cannot stall clicks for the default 6 s. Work triggered from a tap callback that sets frames (the green button) is deferred with `after(0)`.
- Never hit-test a point over ScreenZones' own panels from inside the tap: AX would message this process's main thread, which is blocked in the callback, and wait out the timeout.
- Chromium browsers (Brave, Chrome) stop updating their AX frame when a window-server drag crosses into another display and keep reporting the stale frame, even after activation, until a frame is set through AX. Read through AX alone, a window dropped on another display never left its own, so it is not released, or not adopted, and snaps back on the next placement.
- Turning SIGTERM into `NSApp.terminate` makes LaunchServices launch the app again right after it exits (macOS 27; SIGKILL does not), leaving two instances that fight over the same windows and display. SIGTERM keeps its default action.
- Panels are sRGB and draw only sRGB colors. Every other source/destination color space pair makes CoreGraphics cache a ColorSync transform of three 272 KB tables, the largest share of the footprint; drawing in the display's own color space creates several times as many. Display reconfigurations add pairs for the new profiles, and AppKit's status menu presentation adds about seven the first time the menu opens, so compare footprints with the menu either never opened or already opened.
- On macOS 27 status items are not reachable through the app's `AXExtrasMenuBar`, nor Control Center's; to drive the menu in a test, open it with one real click and press its items through AX. A crowded menu bar hides the item, so click it on a display whose menu bar shows it.
- With "Displays have separate Spaces" on (`spans-displays` 0 in `com.apple.spaces`), a window that crosses a display edge is drawn only on the display that holds most of it, so an oversized window slid past the display's outer edge is clipped there. With it off, that part shows on the neighboring display.
- A minimized window reports the subrole `AXDialog`, not `AXStandardWindow`, so adopting existing windows accepts minimized ones too. Otherwise a window minimized across a restart has no tab and is adopted only when restored, into whatever zone macOS left its frame in.
- `osascript` JavaScript must post the quit notification with `$("…")` and `$.nil`; a plain JS string and `null` post nothing.
- `Info.plist` picks the space-efficient allocator through `LSEnvironment`, which LaunchServices applies only to apps it opens; running the binary directly measures the default allocator.
